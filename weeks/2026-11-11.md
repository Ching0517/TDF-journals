# Nov 11 — Fixing the motion, adding the sensor, linking to p5.js

[← Previous](2026-11-10.md) · [All entries](README.md) · [Next →](2026-11-12.md)

**Goal:** a reliable drop-and-return cycle, triggered by the ultrasonic sensor and connected to
the screen.

## What I did

- **Sound idea:** a quiet classical piece (Tchaikovsky's *October* from *The Seasons*, or the slow
  movement of Vivaldi's *Autumn*) that cuts to silence when the leaf drops and fades back in as the
  visitor paints.
- **Ultrasonic trigger:** the leaf breathes by default; a hand within 5 cm for 1 s makes it drop;
  after 2 s it winds up on the other side and breathes again.
- **Arduino ↔ p5.js over USB (Web Serial):** the Arduino sends `NEAR` when the sensor fires;
  p5.js sends the painted percentage `P0`–`P100`, `C` when complete and `I` when the screen is
  idle. The real leaf now rises as the visitor paints.
- **`TEST` mode:** type `TEST`, then an angle, in the Serial Monitor to calibrate without the
  sensor or p5.js interfering.

## Problems and iteration

- **The leaf swung very low on the second cycle,** and before the next drop it was yanked to the
  top first. Cause: after the free fall the spool kept spinning and wound the line **backwards**,
  so every lift started by unwinding it. Versions tried:
  1. v3 — never leave the teeth (drop by sweeping fast to 35°)
  2. **v4 (kept)** — alternate sides: drop at 0° → lift to 150° → drop at 180° → lift to 30°, the
     original double-gap idea
- **Calibrated on the real gear:** rest at 35° and 145°. The breathing only moves *inward* (16°
  toward the middle), because a symmetric swing at the edge slipped into the gap and dropped the
  leaf by itself.
- **The drop never happened with the sensor.** The log showed `DROP` and `lifting` at the same
  instant. Cause: the loop saved the time *before* reading the sensor, so "time since drop" went
  negative, wrapped around to a huge number and skipped the 2 s wait. Fixed by always reading the
  clock fresh.
- **Connecting:** the p5.serialcontrol app was holding the port, and the p5 web editor flagged the
  serial reader's `while (true)` as an infinite loop. Switched to Web Serial only and rewrote the
  reader without a loop.
- Felt the gears could jam when they re-engage quickly, so I also wrote a version that starts
  lifting from 0°/180° and slows down near the first tooth.
