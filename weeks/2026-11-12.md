# Nov 12 — Making the object hold together and look finished

[← Previous](2026-11-11.md) · [All entries](README.md)

**Goal:** fix the three physical problems in the first full build: the gear came loose, the frame
wobbled, and the top looked unfinished with no place for the sensor.

![First full build](images/2026-11-12_build-front.png)

## What I did

- Translated all on-screen text in the p5.js sketch to English.
- **Gear v2:** the printed sector gear had worked loose on the servo spline. The new gear is two
  laser-cut layers: one with a pocket that holds the servo horn in hot glue, and an outer layer
  with a 0.095" hole for the horn screw.
- **Leg clamps:** half-collars with a semicircle notch that clamp each 1/4" dowel from both
  sides, 3 layers per joint, each layer turned 90°. They go on without taking the legs out. The
  base paper also wasn't glued to the cardboard, which added to the wobble.
- **Top + sensor:** measured my plates (26 cm) and designed a front skirt on the top plate that
  holds the HC-SR04 in two 17 mm holes.

## Iteration on the top skirt

The part with the most versions so far:

1. **Tall living-hinge band** (5.8 cm, full front half) — **snapped** as soon as I bent it; slits
   2.5 mm apart were too stiff.
2. **Seven flat facets** joined with paper — sturdy, but I wanted one piece.
3. **Mock-ups of four tops** to compare: closed round lid, tracing-paper lantern, low band, twig
   crown. The twig crown looked silly, so it was out.
4. **Stacked half rings**, cut down to 160° and nested tightly on the sheet — low (2.6 cm) and
   solid, but many layers.
5. **Final direction:** back to one bent piece, but shorter (140°) and lower (2.8 cm), tapering
   smoothly to 6 mm at the ends. I only have plywood, so I cut small test strips first and chose
   the denser hinge (1.2 mm slits) with the grain parallel to the slits.

**Insight:** a living hinge depends on slit spacing *and* grain direction. Testing a 10 cm strip
first is much cheaper than breaking a full-length piece.
