# Nov 7 — Wave machine experiments and why I left them

[← Previous](2026-11-06.md) · [All entries](README.md) · [Next →](2026-11-08.md)

**Goal:** test whether a one-motor wave machine could drive the leaves.

## What I did

- Looked at a Margolin-style wave and found it too complex to build in time, so I looked for
  ready-made laser-cut wave machines instead.
- Studied a ready-made wave-machine file: a shaft of offset discs, each lifting a slat. Worked out
  that I could cut just the discs and hang fishing lines from them.
- Asked whether running it "in reverse" (lines hanging down instead of slats pushing up) would
  work, and where the lines would attach.
- Re-laid the cut sheet so the leaves are spaced further apart, and flattened it to fit my boards
  (250 × 170 mm and 140 × 140 mm).

![Laser layout of the wave-machine parts on my boards](images/2026-11-07_laser-layout.png)

## Decisions and why

- **Stopped the wave-machine direction.** It makes a nice wave from one motor, but a drop needs
  every line released at once. Releasing lines from the cams would need a clutch or a very tall
  frame for the lines to fall through.
- **Insight:** what I really need is a mechanism that can **wind, wind, then let go, and then take
  control again**. The wave is secondary.
